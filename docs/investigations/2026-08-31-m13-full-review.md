## 1. Verdict

This is a codebase that works, is being actively hardened, and is carrying more undocumented risk than its own documentation admits. Nothing found is prod-breaking or account-compromising: seven independent lanes ran adversarial verification with a refute-by-default stance and produced **zero P0** - the one P0 filed did not survive contact with a probe. What did survive is concentrated in three places that matter commercially. Five of the seventeen P1s put a wrong-magnitude or wrong-currency number in front of a user and label it a genuine Bahrain shelf price, which is the one thing a price-comparison product cannot do; three more show that the async story the repo documents as "AsyncIO throughout / Non-blocking" is false in a way that converts one user's database call into another user's timeout, on a single-worker process whose every budget is wall-clock. The most striking cross-lane signal is not any single defect but the **gap between documented and actual state**: 32 of the 131 confirmed rows, a quarter of the review, are status claims that are simply wrong today: `npm audit` clean (it is 1 critical / 20 high), all flags default OFF (22 are ON, 21 are undocumented), orchestrator ~1,500 lines (7,563), coverage floor 60 (83), ~100 test files (535 / 15,839 nodes), all `console.log` wrapped in `__DEV__` (one is not, and it logs token material). And because CLAUDE.md is what an agent reads first, two of the P1s exist precisely because the documented invariant told everyone the problem was already solved. The frontend's 23 confirmed findings are not a surprise but a prediction fulfilled: it has no blocking automated check of any kind, so its 135-235 Jest suites, its eslint i18n fence and its type checker are all decorative. Where the team has been forced to be careful it has been genuinely careful - the SSRF hardening, the RLS migrations, the strict-currency wave, the 22-site `fire_and_forget` conversion are all real work - and the recurring failure mode is not carelessness but **incomplete propagation**: a correct fix lands on the path where the bug was found and the sibling paths keep the old behaviour. Verification quality was high enough to trust these numbers: every lane ran zero-network reproductions rather than reasoning from source, which is why only two findings died outright and twenty-two were rescored rather than the attrition a review of this size usually shows.

## 4. Themes

Five structural patterns account for most of the 131 rows. Fixing the pattern is worth more than fixing any individual row.

### T1 - Fixed once, never propagated

The dominant failure mode in this codebase is a correct fix applied to the path where the bug was found, with the sibling paths left exactly as they were. The evidence is not anecdotal:

- The strict-currency label wave covers JSON-LD, OG and microdata and **not** WooCommerce (M13-07, M13-08) and **not** the Serper shopping tier (M13-09).
- The Codex SSRF hardening produced `curl_fetch_html_same_site`; 17 adapter fetch sites still follow redirects unvalidated (M13-20), and the bare helper is still fed Serper-supplied URLs.
- `fire_and_forget` converted 22 sites and left one (M13-33).
- `ENABLE_ASYNC_REDIS_OFFLOAD` covers 4 read call sites out of ~111 blocking sites (M13-06); every write and every budget call is still inline.
- The circuit breaker guards Firecrawl and Scrape.do and not Serper (M13-32) - the highest-volume paid provider of the three.
- `parse_price_string` gained a currency and a `display_text` argument on the main path and not in the re-selection stash 100 lines away (M13-10).
- The apex-shadowing suffix scan was written once and copied into at least three modules (M13-12).

**The cheapest review question this repo can adopt: for every fix, who else calls this?** Six of the seventeen P1s would have been caught by asking it.

### T2 - The documentation is a second, wrong source of truth

**32 of the 131 confirmed rows** are status claims in `CLAUDE.md`, `docs/`, or in-code comments that are false on this tree - the full id list is in `synthesis.json` under `totals.documented_status_wrong_ids`, derived rather than asserted, which is the point. This is load-bearing rather than cosmetic because CLAUDE.md is what an agent reads first *by the repo's own instruction*, and because two P1s exist specifically because the documented invariant said the problem was solved: M13-04 (the SSE cap is documented as covering the verdict pipeline; it covers Phase 1) and M13-05 (`AsyncIO throughout / Non-blocking` against 128 synchronous DB calls). The full drift list spans size (`~1,500 lines` vs 7,563), test estate (`~100 files` vs 535 / 15,839 nodes), the coverage floor (60 vs 83), flags (all default OFF vs 22 ON, 21 undocumented), security posture (`npm audit clean` vs 1 critical / 20 high; `all console.log wrapped in __DEV__` vs one that logs token material), the migration ledger (stops at 032), the descriptor store (`robots-allowed rows only` vs 2 of 95 disallowed), the registry count (274 vs 275) and the Sentry version. **Every one of these numbers could be derived rather than asserted** - the single highest-leverage fix in this theme is a CI check that regenerates the claimed figures and fails on drift.

### T3 - Currency is a label, not a type

A price moves through this pipeline as a bare float with a currency string stamped beside it, and every layer that *could* catch a mismatch checks something else. `detect_currency` does not know GCC symbols. `is_price_showable` has a low-price floor, no upper bound, and no currency token anywhere between :1628 and :1752. `response_builder` never compares the price's currency to the request's region. Eight adapters hard-code `"BHD"` and ignore their own `currency` parameter. The conversion table is a frozen 13-currency dict whose unknown-currency path returns a rate of **1.0** with a warning. Five of the seventeen P1s are this one gap, and they compound: M13-08 produces a wrong number, M13-07 labels it genuine, and the `_confirmed()` race then cancels the scraper that would have produced the right one. The structural fix is a `Money` type that carries its currency and cannot be compared or selected across currencies without an explicit conversion - but the buildable version is the response-chokepoint guard in Wave 2.

### T4 - Blocking work on a loop with wall-clock deadlines

Single-worker uvicorn (no `--workers` in Procfile or railway.json), 128 synchronous Supabase `.execute()` calls and ~111 blocking Redis/budget calls inside `async def`, 33 `to_thread` sites all sharing Python's unbounded default executor, and adapter timeouts hardcoded *above* the race that cancels them. Because every budget in the system is wall-clock - the 15s price race, the 25/30s stream cap, the 10s adapter ceiling - one request's blocking call is charged to every concurrent request as a timeout. This theme also explains symptoms the repo has been chasing separately: the free-unit suite hang, cold compares hitting the cap "by design", and Bright Data's 20s fallback that can never return inside a 15s race (M13-34). It is the one theme where the fix is mechanical but broad.

### T5 - Green means nothing on the surfaces that ship to users

No `test` script and no jest job (135-235 suites unexecuted). `tsc` and `npm audit` both `continue-on-error`. `backend-tests` red by construction, so nobody reads it and the coverage floor, the lock-drift gate and the syntax check lose their signal inside the same ignored job. The real regression gate is a local script with an absolute path into one developer's *other checkout*, against three competing baselines of which two exist on one machine. And the sharpest instance: `tests/test_rate_limiting_complete.py` is docstringed "verifying rate limiting on all public endpoints" and its 16 tests touch **none** of the 21 routes that have no rate limit at all - that green is what kept M13-01 invisible. **Tooling that exists but is not wired to a gate is indistinguishable from no tooling**, and this repo has a lot of it.

## 5. Recommended fix waves

Four waves, grouped so each is independently landable and independently revertible, honouring the house dark-ship pattern (new behaviour behind a default-OFF `ENABLE_*` flag; pure defect corrections land unflagged with a pin). Ordering is by exposure-per-unit-of-work, not by severity alone.

### Wave 1 - Front door: authentication, rate limiting and paid-work gating

**Why together:** Every one of these is reachable by an anonymous or token-bearing caller with no feature flag in the way. They share one file cluster (app/main.py, app/middleware/rate_limiter.py, app/api/*_routes.py) so they merge cleanly, and two of them (M13-01, M13-03) are the only findings in the whole review that cost real money or real credentials today.

**Findings:** `M13-01`, `M13-02`, `M13-03`, `M13-21`, `M13-25`, `M13-26`, `M13-28`, `M13-29`

**Scope:** Add SlowAPIMiddleware and audit the 21 undecorated routes; a proxy-aware key_func shared with the audit ip_address field; Depends(verify_admin_key) on /text/price-kpi; an anonymous usage gate keyed on X-Device-Fingerprint for /text/quick and /image/identify; max_length on the POST /text/compare model; the unified error envelope on image_routes.py:267; limiter + Path/Query patterns on the two referral endpoints (both handlers must gain `request: Request`); openapi_url=None under RAILWAY_ENVIRONMENT; UUID validation on the feedback comparison_id.

**Dark flags:**
- ENABLE_PROXY_AWARE_RATELIMIT (default OFF) - wraps the key_func swap so the limiter-key change can be canaried and reverted without a redeploy of the middleware wiring
- ENABLE_ANON_USAGE_GATE (default OFF) - the anonymous freemium gate, so a bad fingerprint heuristic cannot lock out real users

**Deliberately unflagged:** Adding SlowAPIMiddleware, the admin dependency on /price-kpi, the body max_length constraints and the error-envelope fix are the corrections themselves and have no dark-ship form; they land with pins instead.

### Wave 2 - Currency truth: stop shipping a wrong number labelled genuine

## 6. Coverage statement - what this review did NOT cover

**Read this before treating the report as a clean bill of health.** Seven lanes enumerated their own surface and walked it, but the review had hard boundaries.

**Nothing was executed against anything live.** No requests to the deployed Railway service, no paid vendor calls (OpenAI, Serper, Firecrawl, Bright Data, Zyte), no live web fetches. Every reproduction in every lane was zero-network and local. Claims about production behaviour are inferences from source, not observations.

**The test suite was never run as a suite.** Lanes ran `pytest --collect-only` (15,728 of 15,839 free-tier nodes collected) plus targeted zero-network runs: 17 named nodes in tests-ci, the 4 baseline-drift nodes, the 3 SSRF nodes under a DNS blocker, 3 camera_vision nodes. **The true current failure count on this tree is not established** beyond the four nodes M13-17 proves. Anyone acting on M13-17 must re-capture, not assume.

**No database was queried.** Every migration and RLS finding is derived from the `.sql` text and `docs/CONTEXT_DATABASE_API.md`. The policies, foreign-key `ON DELETE` behaviour and PostgREST row caps **as actually applied in the production Supabase** were never inspected - M13-89's cascade conclusion in particular rests on a documented DDL line, not on `pg_constraint`. That one query is in its fix sketch for a reason.

**Frontend types and bundle size were not measured.** `SmartCompareApp/node_modules` is absent from the pinned tree and installing was out of scope, so `npx tsc --noEmit` never ran: the "7 TS errors" figure is a repo comment, not a measurement, and M13-65's bundle claim is inferred from import shape rather than from an `expo export`.
