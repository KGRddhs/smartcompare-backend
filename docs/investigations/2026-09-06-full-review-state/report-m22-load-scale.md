#### (c) After `#114` (per-client limiter) and a second worker — **~8–16 compares/minute at best; ~4.5–5.2/minute if the OpenAI account is Tier 1**

Two independent things happen here, and one of them is a regression unless it is sequenced.

**`#114` removes the accidental guard.** Today every decorated limit is one bucket per (edge peer, concrete URL). Re-keying per client means each client gets its own `10/minute`: 100 users offer up to 1,000 compares/min at a machine that serves 3–8. **`#114` without W0 and without backpressure makes the system strictly worse.** That is the ordering rule (§1e), and it is why `#114` is correctly still held.

**A second worker gives 2 loops** — a real ~2× on the binding per-loop constraints (parse, client construction, DNS) — but it breaks four things, all verified: slowapi's storage is hardcoded `memory://` (`rate_limiter.py:103-110`, runtime-introspected as `limits.storage.memory.MemoryStorage`), so **every rate limit silently doubles**; the Shopify `_next_slot_at` politeness throttle doubles; the per-process observability counters that feed the admin meters under-report by 1/N; and the 40-thread executor becomes 80 threads on the same container. Precondition: `RATELIMIT_STORAGE_URI` + a `rediss://` endpoint + `in_memory_fallback` first (`redis==8.1.0` and `limits==5.8.0` are already pinned at `requirements.txt:136` and `:91`).

**And then OpenAI binds.** MEASURED by driving the real `build_verdict_prompt` over the recorded fixtures with tiktoken's gpt-4o encoding: prompt = **5,498 / 5,614 / 5,794** tokens (finder) and **5,564 / 5,812 / 5,942** (verifier, independent). `extraction_service.py:2447` passes `max_tokens=1000`, which OpenAI's rate limiter charges to TPM at admission. So TPM/verdict ≈ **6,500–6,800**, and at the Tier-1 gpt-4o ceiling of 30,000 TPM that is **4.4–4.6 verdicts/min** — not the runbook's published 6.6 (`docs/runbooks/2026-09-02-openai-tpm-launch-sizing.md:47`). Even discarding the reservation premise entirely (it cannot be tested with a dead key), 30,000 / 5,800 = **5.2/min**, still ~21% below the published figure. Worse, `verdict_chain_max_attempts()` resolves to **6** in prod (MEASURED: both retry knobs absent from the 54 vars) against the 3 the runbook documents as the launch setting, so under saturation the effective rate falls further. **The account tier is UNVERIFIED and verifying it is a hard launch gate**; at Tier 2/3 (450k/800k TPM) this stops binding.

**So the honest (c) answer:** with W0 landed, a Redis-backed limiter, `--limit-concurrency`, a verified per-client anchor and two workers, a realistic MODELLED ceiling is **8–16 compares/min and 4–6 concurrent** — *unless* the OpenAI account is Tier 1, in which case the wall is **~4.5–5.2 compares/min** and nothing the application does moves it. "Hundreds of people at the same time" is not reachable on this architecture without: removing the on-loop work (W0), horizontal replicas with shared limiter storage, a verified OpenAI tier plus a verdict cache, and co-locating Upstash/Supabase — which today are **178 ms** and **285 ms** away from a container running in `asia-southeast1` while OpenAI and Serper are **2–6 ms** away (MEASURED, `RAILWAY_REPLICA_REGION=asia-southeast1-eqsg3a` on 4,742 of 4,742 sampled requests).

### 1d The measurement M18 lacked

M18 recorded "Upstash RTT from Railway NEVER measured" as the biggest unknown in the load model. **It is 178 ms** (median of 10, min 176.2, max 197.1); Supabase is **~285 ms** (median of 10, 131.2–883.6); both offload flags are UNSET — all MEASURED from inside the prod container. Triangulated against the recorded stage timings: 24 of 70 per-product lookup stage values (10/14 `specs_ms` legs, 10/14 `reviews_ms` legs) sit in the 160–210 ms band, i.e. **a cache HIT costs a full blocking round trip, not ~0**. `LS-MEASURED-EVIDENCE-01`. The reason both numbers are large is not slow vendors: the container runs in `asia-southeast1` while OpenAI and Serper are 2–6 ms away — the two dependencies that are *not* co-located are exactly the two that run inline on the loop (`LS-MEASURED-EVIDENCE-10`). Moving the Upstash/Supabase region is a dashboard change and should be measured **before** any code is written against these numbers.

Two other measurement corrections that change how every prior number in this campaign must be read:

- **78.3% of the request log is a synthetic harness** (duration_ms = 0, 0.153 s median inter-arrival, 13 repeating query strings, one user_id slot). Any statistic computed over the raw 13,237-row series is wrong (`LS-MEASURED-EVIDENCE-07`). This is the load lane's mirror of the product lane's `PO-RECORDED-MEASURED-01`.
- **The streaming surface records 0 failures in 721 rows** against 26.0% on the identical non-streaming surface, because the completed-but-client-gone outcome is never written (`LS-MEASURED-EVIDENCE-08`). Streaming reliability has never actually been measured.

### 1e The ORDERING RULE, restated with evidence

**M18's rule:** the offloads must be ACTIVE before the limiter key changes (`#114`), because the mis-keyed limiter is what currently protects the single event loop.

**The rule survives; its premise does not.** Three measurements change it:

1. **The guard is leaky.** Seven decorated routes are keyed on the concrete URL, so rotating a path parameter bypasses the limit entirely — MEASURED 25/25 pass with distinct ids vs 20/25 with one id. The worst of them, `GET /text/prices/{product}`, is unauthenticated, unmetered, and fans out into the paid cascade across six regions. The limiter is *a leaky guard*, not a reliable one, and two items must move **out** of the "wait for the offloads" queue.
2. **The anchor is confirmed; the bucket count is not 1.** All 24 real `admin_audit_log` IPs are Railway edge peers in `100.64.0.0/10` across **10 distinct** addresses (MEASURED over all 1,439 rows). That confirms the trust CIDR `#114` needs — and it means "one bucket deployment-wide" over-constrains the model. The verifier is right that 10 peers accumulated over two months of ~zero traffic does not prove 10 concurrent buckets, so state the ceiling as a range `(paths × limit) × [1..N_peers]`, never as a single number.
3. **`#114` as currently written is a total bypass, not a fix.** `_leftmost_xff_ip` (`rate_limiter.py:33-49`) takes the client-supplied leftmost hop. MEASURED against the real limiter: 40 GETs to a 10/min route while rotating `X-Forwarded-For` → **40×200, 0×429**; with a fixed leftmost → 10×200 then 429. Flipping `ENABLE_PROXY_AWARE_RATELIMIT` as written does not weaken the limiter, it **removes** it, and makes `admin_audit_log.ip_address` attacker-authored. `#114` is correctly HELD.

**Corrected order at `76ace90`:**

- **STEP 0 — does NOT wait on anything.** Land the four unflagged loop blockers (DNS / client reuse + timeouts / Upstash bounded transport / parse offload) and close the guard's holes (route-keyed limiter; meter-or-gate `/text/prices/{product}`). Every one of these only *tightens*; none widens the front door, so the "offloads first" constraint does not apply to them. Add the anchor logging from `LS-RATELIMIT-KEY-09` — read-only instrumentation.
- **STEP 1 — offload flags ACTIVE**, one canary window each, with the Upstash bounded transport already merged (hard precondition) and the M13-31 half-open atomicity precondition already recorded in `CLAUDE.md`. Nothing that raises effective request throughput may precede this.
- **STEP 2 — Redis-backed limiter storage** (`RATELIMIT_STORAGE_URI` + `rediss://` + `in_memory_fallback`), because both a second worker and per-user keys are meaningless while buckets are per-process and restart-wiped. This adds one Upstash round trip — 178 ms, now measured — to every request on 61+ routes; weigh that, do not assume it away.
- **STEP 3 — verified per-client anchor (`#114`)**: run the live hop-count procedure (§3), then apply the winner. This is the first step that changes *who shares a bucket*, so it must not precede STEP 1.
- **STEP 4 — per-user keys (`#72`)**, only after STEP 2 and STEP 3.
- **STEP 5 — `ENABLE_DEFAULT_RATE_LIMITS`**, only now: the exempt list is empty, so a blanket 10/min covers `/health` and the app-open reads, which is survivable only with a per-client key.
- **STEP 6 — raise the limits**, using rate-limit response headers to observe saturation instead of guessing.
- **Orthogonal and free at any point:** `--limit-concurrency` (it only sheds; a 503 at the door beats a 120 s spinner). `--workers 2` only after STEP 2.

### 3.1 The hard limits on this review

1. **No load test, and no burst traffic — HARD RULE 1.** Every concurrency multiplication in §1 is arithmetic on measured per-unit inputs. No queue depth, no thread-pool saturation, no TPM wall and no Upstash hang was observed in a running process under load. The plan to change that is §3.3, and it is **PARKED**.
2. **No live compare exists at `76ace90`.** OpenAI returns `429 insufficient_quota`; the Serper balance is −50. So (b) and (c) in §1a are modelled end to end, and no per-compare figure was ever measured through one real request.
3. **The OpenAI account tier is unverified**, and could not be verified without a working key. The Tier-1 arithmetic in §1b row 4 is conditional on the vendor-published numbers the runbook cites (200K TPM mini / 30K TPM 4o / 500 RPM). At Tier 2/3 that bottleneck stops binding. The gpt-4o-**mini** side of the same table was not re-derived (measuring the mini prompts needs the Serper snippets that feed them, i.e. a live search).
4. **Local pip is OFF-LOCK** (`slowapi` 0.1.9 vs **0.1.10** pinned; `uvicorn` 0.30.0 vs **0.52.4** pinned; `httpx` 0.27.0 vs 0.28.1; `supabase` 2.28.0 vs 2.31.0; `fastapi` 0.115.0 vs 0.141.1), so every finder claim that rested on locally-installed source was re-checked against the **downloaded locked wheel**. Two such re-checks changed an answer and are recorded here because they are easy to inherit wrong: (a) `slowapi` 0.1.10's `extension.py` has **identical** `key_style` / `per_method` semantics to 0.1.9, so the per-URL bucket finding holds at the pinned version; (b) the finder's claim that `--forwarded-allow-ips` cannot take CIDR is **false at the shipping version** — pinned `uvicorn 0.52.4` rewrote `ProxyHeadersMiddleware` around a `_TrustedHosts` class that parses networks with `ipaddress.ip_network` and tests `ip in net`, so `--forwarded-allow-ips=100.64.0.0/10` works; only the local 0.30.0 build does exact-string membership. What survives at 0.52.4 is the *other* trap: with `always_trust`, `get_trusted_client_address` returns `x_forwarded_for_hosts[0]`, the client-supplied leftmost hop. Residual off-lock exposure: any conclusion resting on `fastapi`/`starlette` route introspection, which is why the runtime probes classify on the app's own `run_db`/`to_thread` dispatch rather than on framework shape.
5. **The concurrency-cliff measurement carries a real confound.** Overlap is derived from `duration_ms`, so a slow or failing request mechanically lands in a high-overlap bucket — exposure and outcome share a variable. 6. **The recorded series is 78.3% synthetic.** 13 repeating query strings, 0.153 s median inter-arrival, one user_id slot. Any statistic over the raw 13,237 rows is contaminated; the whole series contains **2 distinct user_id values**, so no per-user load shape exists at all.
7. **Railway observability is thin and expiring.** Railway MCP returned `Unauthorized` for metrics in this session (the CLI has no metrics command), so there is no CPU/RSS time series — only the brief's 24 h baseline and an instantaneous cgroup read. HTTP logs are `METHOD PATH STATUS Nms` only — **no client IP and no forwarded headers**, so the hop count cannot be inferred from them. The June/July window that produced the whole recorded series has no surviving server-side logs.

### 6.3 Confidence in the headline answer

The three answers in §1a rest on different footings and should be trusted differently.

- **(a) TODAY** — the strongest. The per-unit costs are measured through the real runtime (DNS, parse, Upstash RTT, SSL context, command counts), the flag state is measured from the prod variable list, and the latency distribution is measured from 1,892 real rows. The one soft input is the concurrency-response curve (§3.1 item 5). **The "~1 concurrent, ~3/min" conclusion would survive a load test being 50% kinder than modelled**; it would not survive the recorded p50 of 19.9 s being unrepresentative of a restored engine, which is a real risk given the series' age.
- **(b) after the offload flags** — moderate. The *coverage* numbers are measured (21.7%, per-route on-loop counts in both flag states, the on-loop client construction with flags ON). The *throughput* claim is arithmetic and is the least defensible number in this report. Treat "5–8/min" as a hypothesis the parked load test exists to falsify.
- **(c) after `#114` + a second worker** — weakest, and deliberately so. It is conditional on an unverified OpenAI tier, on a `--workers` change whose four breakages are static findings rather than measurements, and on W0 landing first. What is *not* soft in (c) is the ordering: `#114` without W0 and without backpressure makes the system strictly worse, and that follows from measurements alone.

**One sentence for the founder.** The product cannot serve a crowd today — it serves about one comparison at a time and roughly three a minute, and any single unauthenticated request that names a dead hostname can freeze the whole service for eleven seconds — but none of that is the current outage: with OpenAI out of credit and Serper at a negative balance, the machine is currently spending money on every attempt and completing none.
